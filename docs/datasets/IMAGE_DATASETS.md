# Image Dataset Review

Image detection is split into separate tasks. AI-origin classification (a photograph versus a generated image) is not equivalent to face-swap detection, localized image editing, or general-purpose image manipulation detection.

| Dataset | Scale, labels, and methods | Formats and resolution | Access, rights, and storage | Suitability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GenImage | 2,681,167 images in the paper: 1,331,167 real ImageNet images and 1,350,000 generated images. Two primary labels (nature/AI), plus 1,000 content classes and generator provenance. Generators include BigGAN, GLIDE, VQDM, SD 1.4/1.5, ADM, Midjourney, and Wukong. | Compressed archives with per-generator folders. The project does not document one uniform image extension or resolution. The paper describes generator resolutions including 128, 256, and 512 pixels; inspect each authorized file's actual dimensions. | Official repository links Google Drive and Baidu. The dataset terms state CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 plus additional dataset terms and prohibit commercial use. Archive byte size is not published; do not budget by image count alone. | Strong for generator-held-out and degraded-image experiments in AI-origin detection. It is not a face-manipulation benchmark. Do not use its published test split for development. |
| CIFAKE v3 | 120,000 balanced images: 60,000 CIFAR-10 photographs and 60,000 Stable Diffusion 1.4 images. REAL/FAKE; published split is 100,000 train and 20,000 test. | RGB 32x32 JPEG. | Acquired 2026-11-24 from official Kaggle v3: ZIP 109,625,224 bytes; uncompressed payload 110,717,853 bytes. Kaggle's license field says "Other (specified in description)," while its description and author README explicitly state the same MIT license as CIFAR-10. The record requires citing both CIFAKE and CIFAR-10. | All 120,000 files decoded successfully. Exact hashing found 1,336 duplicate pairs; 378 cross the published train/test boundary. Derived duplicate-grouped 80/10/10 splits passed leakage checks at pHash distance 6. Treat these as custom splits, not Kaggle's official test split. |
| FaceForensics++ (image use) | Not a standalone still-image dataset: images are extracted from 1,000 source sequences and their Deepfakes, Face2Face, FaceSwap, and NeuralTextures edits. Labels should retain the specific manipulation method. | Original image/video material and H.264 video at c0, c23, and c40. Resolution varies by source and compression; record dimensions from files. | Access requires an approved request. Terms restrict use to non-commercial research/education. The author README reports about 10 GB for c23 video, 500 GB raw video, 2 GB c40, and about 2 TB for all extracted PNG frames. | Useful face-manipulation baseline and localization material. Split by source video/identity; extracted frames are not independent examples. |
| Celeb-DF v2 (image use) | Frames from 590 celebrity source videos, 5,639 corresponding synthesized videos, and a separate 300-video YouTube-real folder in the released structure. The paper reports more than two million frames. The fake method is an improved face-swap process. | Video-derived frames; exact common resolution/codec is not specified in the author README. Record actual frame dimensions. | Author request forms and terms govern access; do not infer a broad license from the public code repository. No authoritative archive byte size was found. | Cross-dataset face-manipulation evaluation. Do not treat its extracted frames as independent train/test observations. |
| ForgeryNet | 2.9 million face images and 221,247 videos. The paper describes 7 image-level and 8 video-level approaches, real/fake and finer manipulation labels, localization annotations, and 36 perturbations. | Image/video media; a single canonical resolution or archive size was not found in the official README. Inspect the approved release. | Training and validation releases are linked by the authors. The repository's non-commercial notice explicitly covers software; dataset terms must be checked separately before use. Total bytes are not published. | Most useful listed candidate for face-image localization and subtype-aware labels, but too large to approve without a release-level size and terms review. |
| DeeperForensics-1.0 (image use) | Video dataset rather than a still-image collection. It has 48,475 source clips and 11,000 manipulated clips produced with DF-VAE, plus seven perturbation types at five levels and mixed perturbations. | Source videos are 1080p; use extracted frames only with source video IDs retained. | Author request approval and terms of use required. The published full download is about 284 GB and recommends 300 GB free. Its real target videos are not included; the authors point to FaceForensics++ c23. | Useful for robustness development, but its storage footprint and separate real-target dependency make it unsuitable for this workspace's first image pilot. |
